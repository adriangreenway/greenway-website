# Band Video Shoot Plan: 3 to 4 Weddings, One Year of Content

**Created:** 2026-07-27. **Status:** GOAL SHEET. The band EPK and band reel are paused until this footage exists.
**Owner:** Adrian books the videographer and picks the gigs. Claude turns the footage into the reel, EPK, and proposal media afterward.

---

## 1. What we're buying (this is the money-saver)

We are NOT buying a finished edited video. The $3,000 product is mostly editing labor, and we already have our own edit pipeline (the one that cut the solo reel). We are buying:

1. **Raw horizontal footage** of full songs, delivered as files.
2. **Board audio** recorded straight from the console, per song.
3. **Full usage rights** in writing.

Say this to the videographer up front: "Shoot only. No editing, no color grade, no delivery timeline pressure. Raw files and a board recording." That should price well under a full-video package. Anchor to what the micro-content person charged before, and ask for a 3 or 4 gig bundle rate.

**Budget frame (Adrian decides the ceiling):**
- Option A: full production video, ~$3,000 per gig. Rejected as the default, we'd pay for edits we redo ourselves.
- Option B (recommended): one shooter, raw handoff, booked as a multi-gig bundle. One negotiation, one invoice per gig, footage compounds across the season.

## 2. Audio: the pristine part

The whole reason phone footage fails is audio. Per gig:

1. **Board recording, every set.** Whoever runs sound records the full night. Best: multitrack (each mic/instrument separate). Acceptable: a stereo mix of the board. Even a phone-quality board mix beats camera audio.
2. **Camera keeps its own audio too.** We need it for two things: syncing to the board recording, and crowd noise (a board mix sounds dead without the room cheering, we blend the two).
3. **Sync mark:** at the top of each set, one loud count-off or drum click on stage while the camera rolls. That's the clap that lines everything up.
4. Ask the sound engineer to note the song order, or we match it from the set list.

## 3. Master shot wishlist (give this list to the shooter)

All horizontal. 4K if the camera does it, 1080p minimum. Let songs run full length, no cutting mid-song.

**Every gig, non-negotiable:**
- Full band wide shot, everyone in frame, one full song
- Lead vocalist medium shot, one full song
- Packed dance floor WITH the band visible behind it
- Crowd shot from the stage, looking out

**Rotate across the 3 to 4 gigs:**
- Each featured vocalist gets their own showcase song on camera
- Horn section feature moment
- Rhythm section close-ups (drums, bass, guitar, keys)
- First dance or a slow song, the tender counterweight
- Ceremony or cocktail hour set, if we're playing it
- Band intros / the moment the dance floor opens
- Detail shots: hands on instruments, in-ears, the board
- One walk-through shot moving through the crowd toward the stage

**Style notes for the shooter:**
- The band is the subject. Guests are energy, not the story.
- Hold shots. Slow moves beat whip pans, we cut fast in the edit, not in camera.
- Grab 10 seconds of clean venue exterior and room-lit-up shots per gig.

## 6. Rights and permissions

- **Videographer agreement, in writing:** we own the raw footage, perpetual commercial use, all platforms, no watermark, no approval loop on our edits. If they want a credit line, fine.
- **The couple:** the band's client contract already grants reuse of gig media (confirmed 2026-07-23, D14). Confirm that clause covers footage shot by our hired videographer at their event, not just our own phones. If a gig predates that clause, ask the couple directly.
- **Guests in footage:** standard practice, covered by the couple's event, but avoid lingering close-ups of identifiable guests as the subject.
- **Venue names:** only the approved 13 get named publicly. Footage from anywhere is usable unnamed.

## 7. Delivery spec (tell the shooter exactly this)

- Horizontal, 4K preferred, 1080p minimum, highest bitrate their workflow allows.
- Whole clips, unedited, ungraded is fine.
- Board audio as WAV files (or the multitrack session).
- Delivery: drive or download, then everything goes into `~/Desktop/BAND_EPK/gig-1/`, `gig-2/`, etc. (not Downloads).
- Filenames or a note mapping clips to songs.
