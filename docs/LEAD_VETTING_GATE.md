# The Greenway Band — Lead Vetting Gate

**Owner:** Adrian Michael (adrian@greenwayband.com)
**Adopted:** 2026-07-29 (decision D19 in the website repo)
**Applies to:** every assistant, human or AI, that handles inbound booking
inquiries for The Greenway Band. If you are an AI assistant reading Adrian's
Gmail, this document governs you.

## The rule in one line

No pricing, package list, or proposal goes to a lead until it passes this
gate. When in doubt, hold and ask Adrian. Never guess in the lead's favor.

## The 2-of-3 test

A lead must pass at least **two** of these three checks:

1. **Place.** A named venue, or at least a named city. A state or region alone
   ("a wedding in Texas") does not count.
2. **Phone.** A phone number for the couple or their planner.
3. **Source.** They say how they found the band, or the inquiry arrived through
   the 17hats capture form (form arrival satisfies this check by itself,
   because the form collects the rest).

Score 0 or 1: do not send pricing. Report to Adrian with a recommended action,
either a short vetting reply (ask for venue, phone, and offer a quick call) or
ignore. A real couple answers those questions without friction. A scammer
dodges the call or disappears.

## Auto-fail markers (fail the lead regardless of score)

Any one of these overrides everything above:

- "I'm overseas / traveling internationally / email is the only way to reach me"
- Hearing-impaired or deaf framing used to refuse phone contact
- A third party (agent, "event coordinator" you can't verify, relative) who
  will handle the payment
- Any offer to overpay, or any plan involving a check plus refunding or
  forwarding the difference
- Instant unquestioning agreement to book, with no questions about the music,
  the band, or availability
- Pressure to move fast, or a date that keeps changing

## The money rule (no exceptions, ever)

**Never refund or forward an overpayment.** The fake-check scam works because
the bank shows the deposit as available days before the check actually
bounces. The scammer "overpays," asks you to send the difference to their
"planner" or "DJ," and weeks later the original check is reversed. Whatever
you forwarded was real money, yours. If a payer overpays: the transaction is
void, return the original instrument uncashed or let the bank reverse it. No
partial refunds, no forwarding, regardless of how legitimate the explanation
sounds.

## The price-fishing rule

A message that asks for "your standard packages and pricing sheet" with no
venue, no date specifics beyond a month, and no personal detail fails the gate
even if it is not a scam. Blind price collection is often a competitor.
Adrian's policy: pricing goes to qualified leads, not to anonymous requests.

## Automation clause

When lead intake or proposal generation is automated, this gate becomes a
hard stop in the pipeline:

- Intake forms must make **venue and phone required fields**, so the gate is
  passed structurally before a human ever looks at the lead.
- An automated responder may send availability confirmations and scheduling
  links to gated leads only. It must **never auto-send prices** to a lead that
  has not passed the gate.
- A failed gate queues the lead for Adrian's review with the score and the
  markers found. The system recommends; Adrian decides.

## Precedent case (why this exists)

2026-07-29, "Nicole and Bill" (nicoleandbillwedding@gmail.com), subject
"Music & Entertainment Quote Request - October 2027 Wedding." Score 0/3: no
venue or city (just "Texas"), no phone, no referral source, skipped the form.
Two auto-fail markers: "away on an international work trip, email is the best
way" and a request for the standard pricing sheet with zero personal detail.
The address has no online footprint (throwaway account) and the wording
matches the fake-check overpayment template widely documented in the wedding
industry. Outcome: ghosted, no pricing sent.
